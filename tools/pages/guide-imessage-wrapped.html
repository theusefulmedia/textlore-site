  • Terminal gives you a top 10 by phone number or email for free, once Terminal has Full Disk Access.

{{icon:keyboard}}1. Count it free in Terminal

  1. Open System Settings > Privacy & Security > Full Disk Access and switch on Terminal.
  2. Open Terminal and paste this, then press Return:
sqlite3 ~/Library/Messages/chat.db "SELECT h.id, COUNT(*) AS n FROM message m JOIN handle h ON m.handle_id = h.ROWID WHERE m.date/1000000000 + 978307200 >= strftime('%s','2026-01-01') GROUP BY h.id ORDER BY n DESC LIMIT 10;"

You get the 10 phone numbers and emails you swapped the most messages with in 2026. Drop the JOIN and GROUP BY parts to get 1 total. It is free and private. It shows numbers instead of names, counts group chats per person, and makes nothing you can share.

What Textlore doesn't do

  • It is Mac only, and needs macOS 26 or later.
  • It reads the Messages history on your Mac, so your iPhone texts need Messages in iCloud switched on to get there.
  • It reads Messages (iMessage, SMS and the other texts in it) and WhatsApp for Mac. It can't read an iPhone backup.
  • It only reads. It never sends, edits or deletes a message, and nothing leaves your Mac.

{{icon:help}}Questions

Does Apple have an iMessage Wrapped?

No. Messages has no yearly stats. You can count your messages in Terminal or use an app that reads your Messages history, such as Textlore.

How do I see who I text the most?

Run a count query on ~/Library/Messages/chat.db in Terminal for a free list by phone number, or open Textlore's Your Lore for names and share cards.

Does it include my iPhone texts?

Yes, if Messages in iCloud is on for your iPhone and Mac, because your iPhone conversations are then on your Mac too.

Will my share card show my messages?

No. Textlore's cards never show message text, phone numbers, emails or file names, and names are hidden by default.
