{{icon:mail}}1. Ask your lawyer 4 things

  1. Which conversations, and which people?
  2. Which dates?
  3. PDF to read, a spreadsheet to sort, or both?
  4. Do they need the phone itself as well?

Sending only what they ask for keeps their review time, and your bill, down.

{{icon:export}}2. Export exactly that range

Textlore is a Mac app that reads your whole Messages history at once. It only reads: it never sends, edits or deletes a message, and nothing leaves your Mac.

  1. Open the conversation in Textlore and choose the start and end dates.
  2. Choose PDF, plain transcript, timestamps on, date headings on, names in full, phone numbers and emails on.
  3. Export once more as CSV if they want to sort or filter.
{{shot:selecting|(max-width: 960px) calc(100vw - 32px), 760px|Selecting a run of messages in Textlore with Shift-click before exporting.}}

To send only a few key messages with context, click Select, Shift-click to pick a run, and press ⌘E.

{{icon:lock}}3. Send it safely

  • Use the secure upload link your lawyer's office gives you, if they have one.
  • Name the file clearly: who, which dates, and the day you exported it.
  • Keep your own copy, and keep the original conversation and phone.

{{icon:help}}Questions

What format do lawyers want for text messages?

Most ask for a PDF that shows who sent each message and when. Some also want a CSV to sort. Ask yours first.

Can I send a group chat?

Yes. Textlore exports group chats with every sender's name, and their number if you switch numbers on.

Can I leave out other people's numbers?

Yes. Phone numbers and emails are hidden by default, so you choose when to show them.

Do I need to send photos too?

Ask your lawyer. Textlore can include photos full size, small, or not at all.

Is Textlore a forensic tool?

No. It makes clear, dated copies from your own Mac. If your lawyer needs a forensic extraction from the phone, they will use a specialist.
