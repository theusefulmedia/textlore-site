How to export iMessages to CSV or Excel on a Mac, 3 ways.

A spreadsheet of your texts is handy for a case, an expense claim, research or just counting who texts more. Messages can't make one, but your Mac can, for free, if you don't mind Terminal.

Updated 21 October 2026. For Messages on macOS 26 and later.

On this page

Quick answer 1. sqlite3 in Terminal 2. Backup tools 3. Textlore Questions

Quick answer

Messages has no CSV or Excel export. The free way is to copy the Messages database and export a conversation with the sqlite3 tool built into macOS, which writes a CSV file that opens in Excel or Numbers. For a chat with years of history, or if you'd rather not use Terminal, an export app is faster.

1. Export a conversation to CSV with sqlite3

Messages stores your history in a database file, chat.db, in your Library folder. macOS includes sqlite3, a tool that can read it and write the results as CSV. You'll paste 3 commands.

  1. Open System Settings > Privacy & Security > Full Disk Access and switch on Terminal. macOS protects the Messages folder, and Terminal can't read it without this.
  2. Open Terminal and paste this, so you work on a copy and never on the live file:
mkdir -p ~/Desktop/messages-copy && cp ~/Library/Messages/chat.db* ~/Desktop/messages-copy/
  1. List your conversations. For a one-to-one chat the identifier is the phone number or email. Group chats show the name you gave them in the display_name column.
sqlite3 -header ~/Desktop/messages-copy/chat.db "SELECT chat_identifier, display_name FROM chat;"
  1. Paste this, with +15551234567 replaced by the identifier from the last step. It writes messages.csv to your desktop with the date and time, who sent each message, and the text.
sqlite3 -header -csv ~/Desktop/messages-copy/chat.db "SELECT datetime(m.date/1000000000 + 978307200, 'unixepoch', 'localtime') AS sent, CASE m.is_from_me WHEN 1 THEN 'Me' ELSE h.id END AS sender, m.text FROM message m JOIN chat_message_join cm ON cm.message_id = m.ROWID JOIN chat c ON c.ROWID = cm.chat_id LEFT JOIN handle h ON h.ROWID = m.handle_id WHERE c.chat_identifier = '+15551234567' ORDER BY m.date;" > ~/Desktop/messages.csv
  1. Double-click messages.csv to open it in Numbers, or open it from Excel. Delete the messages-copy folder when you're done, since it holds your whole history.

Where it falls short

  • Some rows have no text. On recent versions of macOS, many messages keep their text in a coded column instead of the plain text one, so those rows come out blank.
  • Numbers, not names. The sender column shows phone numbers and emails, not the names in your Contacts.
  • No attachments. Photos and files aren't in the CSV, and reactions show up as their own rows.
  • Easy to get wrong. One missing quote mark and the command fails, and the date maths is easy to break if you edit it.

2. Use an app that reads iPhone backups

If your texts are only on your iPhone, or you use Windows, apps that read iPhone backups can export them as a spreadsheet. iMazing exports messages to Excel and CSV, and Decipher TextMessage exports CSV. Both are paid and run on Mac and Windows.

  1. Install the app and connect your iPhone. Let the app make or read a backup of the phone.
  2. Open the messages section in the app and choose the conversation.
  3. Choose the CSV or Excel export and save the file.

The free command-line tool imessage-exporter reads backups too, but it exports text and HTML files, not CSV.

Questions

Can I export iMessages to Excel for free?

Yes, with sqlite3 in Terminal, which comes with macOS. It writes a CSV file that Excel and Numbers open. It takes some care, and on recent macOS some messages come out without their text.

Why are some messages blank in my CSV?

Newer versions of macOS keep the text of many messages in a coded column of the database instead of the plain text column. A simple sqlite3 query only reads the plain column, so those rows are empty. Export apps decode both.

Can I export iMessages from my iPhone to Excel?

Switch on Messages in iCloud so your iPhone texts are on your Mac, then use any method on this page. Or use an app that reads iPhone backups, such as iMazing.

Can I export a group chat to CSV?

Yes. In sqlite3, use the group's chat_identifier from the list of conversations. In Textlore, open the group and export it like any other conversation.

Does exporting change anything in Messages?

No. sqlite3 reads a copy of the database, and Textlore only reads too. It never sends, edits or deletes a message, and nothing is uploaded.
