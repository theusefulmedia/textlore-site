How to print text messages for court from a Mac, 3 ways.

When texts become evidence, a tidy printout matters: who sent each message, when, and the conversation around it. These are the ways to print them from a Mac, starting with the free one.

Updated 21 October 2026. For Messages on macOS 26 and later.

Before you print anything

A few habits make texts much easier to use later, whichever way you print them.

  • Ask what they want. Your lawyer or the court clerk can tell you the format they accept, whether they want paper or PDF, and whether they need the phone itself.
  • Keep the originals. Don't delete the conversation, and don't edit or crop out messages you don't like. Keep the iPhone, and set Messages > Settings > General > Keep messages to Forever so nothing is removed automatically.
  • Show who and when. A useful printout makes clear which phone number or email each message came from, and the date and time of each one.
  • Include the context. Print the messages before and after the key ones, not a single bubble on its own.

1. Print the conversation from Messages

Messages on your Mac prints a conversation the way it looks on screen, and the same dialog can save a PDF.

  1. If the texts are on your iPhone, check that Messages > Settings > iMessage > Enable Messages in iCloud is on and the Mac has finished syncing.
  2. Open Messages and click the conversation. Note down the person's phone number or email: click the name at the top of the conversation, or look them up in Contacts.
  3. Scroll up until the first message you need is on screen. Messages loads older texts in batches as you scroll.
  4. Choose File > Print, or press ⌘P. Print on paper, or open the PDF menu and choose Save as PDF.

Where it falls short

  • Times are not on every message. Messages shows the time as a heading between groups of messages, so the printout may not show exactly when each text was sent.
  • Names can hide numbers. If the person is in your Contacts, Messages shows their name, so their number may not appear on the page. Write it on a cover page, or ask your lawyer how to show it.
  • No date range. You get everything from your scroll point down to today. Remove pages you don't need in Preview afterwards.
  • Photos are hit and miss. Some print small, some split across pages, and photos kept only in iCloud may not print.

2. Take screenshots that show the number and time

Screenshots are fine for a few messages, as long as each one shows who sent it and when.

  1. On your iPhone, open the conversation and tap the name at the top so the phone number or email shows. Take a screenshot of it.
  2. Swipe left on the conversation to show the time next to each message. Take screenshots of the messages you need, including the ones around them.
  3. AirDrop the screenshots to your Mac, select them in Finder, Control-click and choose Quick Actions > Create PDF. Check the pages are in order.

Where it falls short

  • Easy to question. A cropped image is simple to fake, so the other side may challenge it.
  • Slow for long chats. Months of messages turn into dozens of images.

Questions

Will a court accept printed text messages?

Courts often accept printed texts, but the rules differ by court and country, and the other side can challenge them. Ask your lawyer or the court clerk what they need before you print.

How do I show the date and time of each text?

Messages shows times only between groups of messages. On an iPhone, swipe left in a conversation to show each time and take screenshots. Textlore can include timestamps and date headings in the PDF.

How do I show the phone number instead of the contact name?

Look up the number in Contacts, or by clicking the name at the top of the conversation, and add it to a cover page. In Textlore, switch on phone numbers and emails in the export options; they are hidden by default.

Can I print only the messages from certain dates?

Not directly in Messages. You can print everything from the start date down and remove pages in Preview, or choose a date range when you export in Textlore.

Should I delete the texts after printing them?

No. Keep the original conversation and the phone. Your lawyer may need them to show the printout is real.
