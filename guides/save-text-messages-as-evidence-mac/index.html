How to save text messages as evidence on a Mac, before anything is lost.

If texts might matter in a dispute, the first job is to stop them disappearing. The second is a clean copy that shows who sent each message and when. Here is the order to do it in.

Updated 24 November 2026. For Messages on macOS 26 and later.

Quick answer

Turn off auto-delete in Messages on your Mac and iPhone first. Keep the phone and the original conversation. Then export the conversation as a dated PDF that shows the phone number and time of every message. This guide is practical help, not legal advice. Ask your lawyer or the court what they accept, because rules differ between courts and countries.

1. Stop the texts being deleted

Messages can delete old texts on its own. Switch that off on every device before you do anything else.

  1. On your Mac, open Messages > Settings > General and set Keep messages to Forever.
  2. On your iPhone, open Settings > Apps > Messages > Keep Messages and choose Forever.
  3. Leave Messages in iCloud on, so the Mac has the same history as the phone.

Don't delete the conversation, and don't edit or crop out messages you don't like. A complete record is worth more than a tidy one.

2. Know what a useful copy shows

  • Who sent each message. The phone number or email, not only a contact name.
  • When. The date and time of every message.
  • Context. The messages before and after the key ones.
  • Photos and files that were part of the conversation.

Messages on a Mac prints times only between groups of messages, and shows names in place of numbers. That's why a plain printout often falls short.

3. Export a dated PDF with Textlore

Textlore is a Mac app that reads your whole Messages history at once. It only reads: it never sends, edits or deletes a message, and nothing leaves your Mac.

  1. Install Textlore and switch on Full Disk Access when it asks. About 100,000 messages import in under a minute.
  2. Open the conversation and choose the date range, or press ⇧⌘E for the whole chat.
  3. Pick PDF and the plain transcript. Turn on timestamps and date headings, and switch on phone numbers and emails. They are hidden by default.
  4. Check the live preview, then export. Send the PDF to your lawyer, or print it.

Export the same range as CSV too if your lawyer wants to sort the messages in Numbers or Excel.

4. Keep a second copy safe

  • Save the PDF in 2 places, for example your Mac and an external drive.
  • Keep Textlore's archive on. It keeps its own copy of every message and every photo that reaches the Mac, even if Messages later deletes or offloads them.
  • Note the date you made the export in the file name, like Texts with J, Jan to Nov 2026, exported 24 Nov.pdf.

Questions

Can I use text messages as evidence?

Often yes, but it depends on the court and country, and the other side can challenge them. Ask your lawyer what format they need.

Do screenshots count as evidence?

They can, but cropped images are easy to question. A full export that shows numbers and times is harder to dispute.

Will exporting change the messages?

No. Textlore only reads a copy of your Messages database. It never edits, sends or deletes a message.

What if I already deleted some texts?

Check Messages on every device you use, and Recently Deleted in Messages, which keeps deleted texts for up to 30 days. Your lawyer can advise on other ways to get them.

Does my data leave my Mac?

No. Textlore makes no network calls with your messages. The export file stays wherever you save it.
